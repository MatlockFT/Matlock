**Preston LaGrange** is a 6'3" middleweight moving up to light heavyweight on roughly two weeks' notice. He trains under Keoni Koch at Hard Drive Performance Center in Iowa and comes from a wrestling and football background. His MMA game reflects that. He wants to make fights physical, push a high pace, get into grappling exchanges and hunt finishes rather than manage rounds. All six of his professional wins have come inside the distance, with four submissions. His last two wins were rear-naked chokes over Derek Benoit and Dylan Diebitz. The obvious concern is the size jump and the short notice. His only professional loss also came in a grappling scramble against Brandon Holmes, where LaGrange had a triangle threatening before Holmes lifted him, slammed him hard and finished with punches. LaGrange is dangerous in those chaotic positions, but that loss is a reminder that he can put himself in vulnerable spots while attacking.

**Nell Ariano** is much more seasoned than a 6-0 record makes him look. He is a judo black belt who spent nearly a decade with the French national team, won an IMMAF European title and now trains under former Bellator champion Christian M'Pumbu at Platinum MMA. At 6'4", he is a genuinely large light heavyweight with explosive throws, heavy hands and a strong top game. Four of his six professional wins are knockouts, and three of those finishes came in the second or third round, so his power has held up as fights extend. His résumé is also stronger than LaGrange's. He has gone 6-0 entirely under the Cage Warriors banner and has wins over Riku Urholin, Charles Joyner, Danny Hartwell and most recently Norbert Pietrzak. Cage Warriors specifically described him as having heavy hands, explosive throws and a suffocating ground game.

This is a pretty unusual short-notice matchup because LaGrange is not just taking a late replacement fight, he is going up a division to face someone who is already huge for 205 and has a real judo base. LaGrange should have the speed advantage and may be the more natural pressure wrestler, but Ariano is the more proven light heavyweight and has already shown he can fight effectively deep into three rounds.

**Ryuho Miyaguchi** is a long 5'10" bantamweight from Osaka who trains at MMA Dojo Cobra. He is a karate-based striker with six knockouts in seven wins and does most of his best work with explosive kicks and sudden entries from range. Before his first professional loss, he had stopped six opponents and then beat former UFC fighter Teruto Ishihara by unanimous decision, which is easily the most recognizable win on either man's résumé. Miyaguchi's loss came in May in the opening round of Road to UFC, where Chungreng Koren submitted him with a rear-naked choke. UFC's own preview says Miyaguchi felt overwhelmed by the size of that stage and believes nerves affected his performance. Whether that was the main problem or not, the fight still showed that his striking becomes much less comfortable when an opponent can force prolonged grappling and take his back.

**Mateus Soares** is only 22 but already has 13 professional fights. He trains out of Rizzo RVT and is a protégé of Pedro Rizzo, with a striking base that blends Muay Thai and karate. He is only around 5'4" to 5'5", but he fights aggressively and has real finishing power, with nine knockouts and 10 total finishes in 11 wins. He has also shown he is not helpless on the mat, owning a heel-hook submission win earlier in his career. His two losses are a decision and a D'Arce choke loss to Mackson Lee, meaning nobody has knocked him out as a professional. Since the Lee loss he has won four straight, including a five-round decision over Derick Borges, a first-round stoppage of previously unbeaten Luiz Pacífico and a first-round TKO over veteran Edson dos Anjos.

This fight has a huge geometry difference. Miyaguchi is roughly five or six inches taller and wants space for the kicking game, while Soares is the shorter pressure striker who has been comfortable closing distance and exchanging. Miyaguchi has the cleaner long-range weapons, but Soares has more professional rounds and has faced several opponents with winning records.

**Alivia Bierley** is 5-1 as a professional after an unbeaten amateur run, and she trains under UFC veteran Pete Spratt at Spratt Hybrid Systems in Texas. She was a college basketball player and gymnast before moving into combat sports, and her game is built around physical strength, pressure and being comfortable in ugly exchanges. Her amateur résumé is important because she was much more submission-oriented there, scoring multiple chokes before turning professional. As a pro, the damage has increasingly come from her hands. She has won five straight since losing a split decision in her debut to Maya Samuels-Stewart, and her last two fights ended by knockout against Shanna Gomez and by third-round TKO against Nikola Kacperska. **Summer Onley** is 6-0 and the reigning Full Contact Contender bantamweight champion out of Ronin MMA in England. She started MMA relatively late but had a substantial amateur career before turning pro, including representing England at the 2022 IMMAF European Championships and taking silver. She stopped unbeaten Mônica Fontes to win the FCC title and then defended it by stopping Myllena Messias, a dangerous Brazilian finisher, early in the second round.

The records make this look like striker versus striker, but both women have much more grappling experience than their professional finish columns suggest. Bierley is probably the more physically imposing clinch fighter, while Onley has the cleaner grappling résumé and has been finishing better opposition lately.
