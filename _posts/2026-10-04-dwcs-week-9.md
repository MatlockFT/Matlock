**[Preston LaGrange](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/353189-preston-lagrange)** is a 6'3" middleweight moving up to light heavyweight on about two weeks' notice. He trains under [Keoni Koch](https://harddriveperformance.com/about-us/) at [Hard Drive Performance Center](https://harddriveperformance.com/about-us/) in Iowa and has a wrestling and football background. He likes making things physical, pushing the pace, getting into grappling exchanges and looking for a finish. All six of his pro wins are finishes, including four submissions. His last two wins were rear-naked chokes over [Derek Benoit](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/307039-derek-benoit) and [Dylan Diebitz](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/244957-dylan-diebitz). His only pro loss came against [Brandon Holmes](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/336210-brandon-holmes) in a crazy grappling exchange. LaGrange had a triangle going, Holmes picked him up, slammed him and finished him with punches. LaGrange attacks submissions aggressively and that exchange shows some of the risk that comes with it.

**[Nell Ariano](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/324885-nell-ariano)** is 6-0 with a lot of experience behind that record. He's a judo black belt who spent close to a decade with the French national team, won an IMMAF European title and now trains under former Bellator champion [Christian M'Pumbu](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/christian-mpumbu-tonton) at [Platinium MMA Training Center](https://boxemag.ouest-france.fr/club/platinium-mma-training-center/). He's 6'4" and big for light heavyweight. Four of his six wins are knockouts. Three of those finishes came in the second or third round. All six of his pro fights have been in Cage Warriors, with wins over [Riku Urholin](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/334282-riku-urholin), [Charles Joyner](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/197201-charles-joyner), [Danny Hartwell](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/350325-danny-hartwell) and [Norbert Pietrzak](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/319820-norbert-pietrzak).

LaGrange is taking this fight late and moving up to 205. Ariano is already a big light heavyweight, has a legit judo background and has fought the stronger schedule.

I might be crazy for thinking this, but seeing LaGrange at +325 seems way off the mark. Nell Ariano may look scarier, be more athletic and have the judo chops, but the guy has horrid footwork and pretty terrible fight IQ. He's way over his skis when he throws punches and crashes into the clinch. He'll use his judo to toss his opponents, but he gets out of position almost immediately if he cannot land damaging blows. He looks like a baby deer learning to walk.

This works in Preston's favor. He can range find with the 1 and land the 2 as Nell comes in. He doesn't really need an intercepting counter, either. Nell finds himself out of position after throwing punches all the time, so LaGrange can eat those and throw back. The terrible thing for Preston, though, is that he has virtually no head movement or striking defense to speak of. So despite Nell being as sloppy and Flintstone-footed as he is, he may only need one shot to put LaGrange out.

LaGrange usually fights at middleweight and often outsizes his opponents. I do think he has power, and he stays fairly flat-footed because he knows it only takes "one shot" to put his opponents' lights out. If I'm judging the two strictly on their striking technique, I think LaGrange is a bit more sound in how he puts together his striking game, even if it is super meat and potatoes with no defense. Nell Ariano is kinda wacky and uncomfortable.

I also don't really favor Nell Ariano on the mat. I think Preston is much more opportunistic, even if it can be to his detriment. I could easily see Preston getting a rear-naked choke in this fight, as Nell has shown time and time again that he gives up his back. The judo has not been very impressive, in my opinion.

If I'm truly trying to understand the odds, it has to be that LaGrange's most high-profile fight is him getting slam KO'd. Nell looks scary and has judo credentials. LaGrange isn't very athletic and gets outwrestled quite easily. In all likelihood, that will be the determining factor in this fight. I think most people are assuming that Nell, being more athletic and larger, will be able to take whatever Preston throws at him and tough it out. I'd anticipate people think Ariano gets LaGrange in the clinch, hits a toss or takedown, gets him to the mat and finishes him with ground and pound. I can see that, for sure. However, I do not see it as strongly as the odds indicate at all. I'd put this fight closer to Ariano being a 2-to-1 favorite.

**[Ryuho Miyaguchi](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/373900-ryuo-miyaguchi)** is a very tall bantamweight at 5'10" and trains at [MMA Dojo Cobra](https://www.tapology.com/gyms/1927-cobra-kai-mma-dojo) in Osaka. He's a karate-based striker with six knockouts in seven wins. His kicks and range are the main parts of his striking. Before his first pro loss, he stopped six opponents and then beat former UFC fighter [Teruto Ishihara](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/22921-akihito-ishihara-yashabo) by decision. That's the best name either guy has beaten. His loss came in May in Road to UFC when [Chungreng Koren](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/251434-chungreng-koren) got his back and submitted him with a rear-naked choke. Miyaguchi later said nerves played a part. Koren was still able to get through his kicking range, force the grappling and take his back.

**[Mateus Soares](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/296193-mateus-soares)** is 22 with 13 pro fights already. He trains out of [Rizzo RVT](https://www.tapology.com/gyms/9258-rizzo-rvt) under [Pedro Rizzo](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/pedro-rizzo-the-rock) and has a Muay Thai and karate background. His two losses are a decision and a D'Arce choke to [Mackson Lee](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/242083-mackson-lee). Nobody has knocked him out. He's won four straight since the Lee loss, including a five-round decision over [Derick Borges](https://www.sherdog.com/fighter/Derick-Garcia-Borges-232383) and first-round finishes over [Luiz Pacífico](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/events/140667-brtl-11) and [Edson dos Anjos](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/137333-edson-souza-mo-nervosa).

I'm not impressed by Ryuho Miyaguchi's overall game. There isn't anything he does particularly well. His footwork leaves a lot to be desired, and he hasn't shown much in terms of wrestling or grappling from what I've seen. I just have trouble identifying what his overall goal is going to be against higher-caliber competition because standing at open range and knocking fools out isn't gonna happen for him in the UFC. I don't think it happens against Soares either.

What jumps off the page about Soares is the fact that he trains outta Rizzo RVT under Pedro Rizzo, which is super cool. Aside from that, he's young and I think his game is much more well-rounded. He's primarily a counter striker, but I think he could really benefit from pressuring his opponents more. He doesn't feint, jab, or level change very often, at least from what I've seen. That can lead to him just throwing kicks or jumping knees from very far out. It also doesn't lend itself to a good fight if his opponent is evasive and sticking and moving.

There is a world where Soares fights someone who doesn't engage in the pocket and instead does most of their work at kicking range. Think an Ian Garry type. From what I've seen, Soares doesn't have much of an answer for that right now. However, he's young, and I think fight by fight we'll start seeing those improvements. Miyaguchi kinda has that style, but he's much more willing to engage.

I see Soares landing heavy leg kicks and counter shots, catching Miyaguchi's kicks and working him on the mat to get to dominant positions. Miyaguchi is definitely tough, and if he minds his Ps and Qs, he could possibly stick and move his way to a decision. I just don't see that happening.

Soares looks pretty good, and I think he's a level above Miyaguchi. I think he'll fit in well in the UFC and, with time, probably has top-ten potential.

**[Alivia Bierley](https://www.sherdog.com/fighter/Alivia-Bierley-392070)** is 5-1 as a pro after an unbeaten amateur run and trains under UFC veteran [Pete Spratt](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/pete-spratt-secret-weapon) at [Spratt Hybrid Systems](https://www.tapology.com/gyms/8649-pete-spratt-muay-thai) in Texas. She played college basketball and did gymnastics before getting into fighting, and you can see the athletic background. She's strong, likes pressure and doesn't seem bothered by ugly clinch exchanges. As an amateur, she won several fights by choke. As a pro, she's done more of her damage with her hands. She's won five straight since losing a split decision in her debut. Her last two ended with a knockout of [Shanna Gomez](https://www.sherdog.com/fighter/Shanna-Gomez-398218) and a third-round TKO of [Nikola Kacperska](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/fighters/293859-nikola-kacperska). She's already been through a few full three-round fights and also had a five-round amateur title fight.

Bierley has several amateur submission wins, but Onley is a BJJ brown belt with high-level amateur experience and the better grappling credentials. Bierley seems stronger in the clinch, while Onley has been finishing better opponents lately. They're the same listed height. The clinch and scrambles are probably going to matter a lot.

What turns me off about Bierley is she has little nuance to her game and defaults to what I will now refer to as the WMMA blitz. We've all seen it before if you've spent any length of time watching WMMA. There is some play at range, then whether there is an opening or not, they blast forward with 1-2-1-2-1-2 until they hit a clinch, takedown, or whatever happens next. Ronda Rousey made an entire career of welcoming those blitzes and tossing girls on their ass.
