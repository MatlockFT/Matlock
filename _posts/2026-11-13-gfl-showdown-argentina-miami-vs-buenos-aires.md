The [Global Fight League](https://www.tapology.com/fightcenter/events/150123-gfl-miami-vs-buenos-aires) is scheduled to hold its first event on Friday, December 18, 2026, at Microestadio Antonio Rotili, inside Club Atlético Lanús in Buenos Aires, Argentina. The Buenos Aires Gauchos will take on the Miami Monsters in a ten-fight team competition.

The league originally made headlines in 2025 by drafting established fighters and former UFC champions, then canceled its planned May launch events. Its return comes with a different-looking roster. The December card is built largely around regional professionals, with former Bellator fighter Grant Neal and former UFC fighter Omar Morales among the best-known names. Marlon Moraes was announced for Miami earlier this fall but is not booked on the opening card.

GFL announced the matchups on November 9. **The list below is not an official fight order**, and the promotion has not confirmed a broadcast or streaming partner. The tale-of-the-tape graphics use the records published with the announcement, not independently checked career statistics. [GFL announcement coverage](https://www.mmafighting.com/gfl/520221/gfl-reveals-first-official-fight-card-for-dec-18) · [Event ticketing](https://www.passline.com/eventos/global-fight-league-buenos-aires-gauchos-v-miami-monsters)

## What is the GFL?

The idea is simple: individual fights contribute to a team result. Instead of ten independent winners making up a conventional card, Buenos Aires and Miami are competing across ten weight limits for the overall team victory. The league's ticketing materials describe this as the opening matchup in an eight-team elimination tournament, with the rest of the bracket expected to take place in 2027.

GFL's previously published scoring system awards **four points for a finish, three for a decision win, two for a draw, one for a decision loss, and zero for a stoppage loss**. That would make a finish more valuable than a decision victory in the team standings. The promotion still needs to confirm whether that exact scoring system is being used at the December event. [Original scoring explanation](https://www.sherdog.com/news/news/Global-Fight-League-Reveals-TeamBased-Format-Roster-with-Multiple-ExUFC-Champs-195692) · [GFL tournament roadmap](https://invest.mmagfl.com/)

The weight divisions are also different from the league's original proposal. Heavyweight and light heavyweight are absent, leaving **185 pounds as the highest limit**. The card also has 165-, 160-, and 150-pound bouts and two women's contests at 110 and 105 pounds. Those limits should not be mistaken for the UFC's usual weight-class structure.

## What still needs to be confirmed

The announced lineup is a starting point, not the finished event. The GFL still has to publish an official fight order and viewing information. The event's ticket listing also differs from the November 9 announcement on Eduardo Cajal–Jacob Diaz's contracted weight: the announcement says **150 pounds**, while Passline says **155 pounds**. Passline uses a different spelling for Ethan Burmingham and omits a draw from Pamela Mara's published 7-4-1 record. These details should be checked again before publication.

The more significant question is whether GFL can finally stage the event. The promotion already canceled two planned 2025 shows after announcing high-profile signings. The December card is much smaller in name recognition but provides a concrete lineup for its proposed team format.
