UFC 334: Gane vs. Hokit takes place Saturday, November 14, at Madison Square Garden in New York City. The early prelims begin at 5 p.m. ET, followed by the prelims at 7 p.m. ET and the main card at 9 p.m. ET. The card streams on Paramount+.

Ciryl Gane defends the heavyweight title against unbeaten challenger Josh Hokit in the five-round main event. The co-main pairs women's bantamweight champion Kayla Harrison with returning former two-division champion Amanda Nunes. The rest of the 12-fight card includes Caio Borralho against Yousri Belgaroui, Uroš Medić against Kevin Holland, and several matchups between established veterans and newer UFC names.

**Ciryl Gane** is a technically skilled heavyweight striker with a Muay Thai background, known for his footwork, speed and ability to fight from either stance. He uses feints, jabs, front kicks and low kicks to manage distance, often drawing opponents forward before stepping out of range and countering. Rather than standing his ground and trading heavy punches, Gane prefers to stay mobile, attack from different angles and gradually break opponents down with accurate, well-timed strikes.

**Josh Hokit** is a former collegiate All-American wrestler who has developed into an aggressive, high-volume heavyweight striker. He pushes forward, throws punches in long combinations and is willing to absorb shots to keep his offense going. Despite his wrestling credentials, Hokit has shown a preference for standing and trading punches, using his physical strength, relentless pace and durability to overwhelm opponents rather than relying primarily on takedowns.

**Kayla Harrison** is a two-time Olympic gold medalist in judo who has built her MMA career around physical strength, clinch work and dominant top control. She uses body locks, trips and throws to bring opponents to the ground, where she applies heavy pressure, advances position and attacks with ground-and-pound or submissions. Her striking is still developing and primarily serves to close distance and set up her grappling, where she is most dangerous.

**Amanda Nunes** is a former two-division UFC champion and one of the most accomplished finishers in women's MMA history. She's an explosive, heavy-handed striker who builds much of her offense around a powerful right hand, sharp boxing combinations and low kicks. Although best known for her knockout power, Nunes is also a Brazilian jiu-jitsu black belt with strong wrestling, effective takedowns and punishing ground-and-pound. She can fight aggressively when she senses an opening, but she's equally capable of slowing things down, controlling position and winning rounds without relying on a finish.

**Luis Gurule** is a pressure-heavy flyweight who relies on a high work rate, conditioning and physical strength to wear opponents down. He moves forward behind combinations and kicks, mixing in clinch work and upper-body takedowns when he gets close. He's also a capable scrambler who is difficult to control on the ground. Gurule is at his best when he can maintain a steady pace and force opponents into prolonged exchanges, although his aggressive approach can leave him open to counters.

**Bilal Hasan** is an undefeated flyweight striker with a third-degree black belt in Taekwondo, combining an unconventional kicking game with increasingly effective boxing. He switches stances, attacks the body and uses quick combinations and counterpunches to create openings for his power shots. Despite his Taekwondo background, Hasan has developed into a dangerous puncher, with eight of his ten professional victories coming by knockout or TKO. He's also trained in wrestling and Brazilian jiu-jitsu, although his striking remains the strongest and most proven part of his game.

**Caio Borralho** is a well-rounded Brazilian middleweight and member of the Fighting Nerds team, combining a strong grappling background with a patient, technical striking style. He primarily fights from a southpaw stance, using feints, straight punches, kicks and lateral movement to control distance and create openings. A Brazilian jiu-jitsu black belt with a background in judo, Borralho is also an effective wrestler who uses explosive takedowns to establish top control, advance position and look for submissions. He's a measured, adaptable fighter who is comfortable spending entire rounds on the feet or switching to his grappling when the matchup calls for it.

**Yousri Belgaroui** is a Dutch-Tunisian middleweight and former GLORY kickboxer who challenged for the promotion's world title three times before transitioning to MMA. Standing 6'6", he's an experienced, technical striker who uses his height and reach to establish a stiff jab, punish opponents with low kicks and attack with knees when they close the distance. Belgaroui is particularly effective at breaking opponents down over several rounds, maintaining a steady output while accumulating damage rather than relying on a single knockout punch. He trains alongside Alex Pereira and Glover Teixeira, where he's continued developing his wrestling defense and ability to get back to his feet.

**Uroš Medić** is a Serbian welterweight with an accomplished kickboxing background and some of the most dangerous finishing power in the division. A southpaw, he uses sharp combinations, straight left hands, hooks and powerful kicks to attack opponents at different ranges. He's an aggressive, accurate striker who looks to hurt opponents early and rarely allows them time to recover once he has them in trouble. His biggest vulnerabilities have come in grappling exchanges, where stronger wrestlers have been able to control and submit him. Medić has finished all 14 of his professional victories, 12 by knockout or TKO.

**Kevin Holland** is a tall, unorthodox welterweight striker with an 81-inch reach, fast hands and a background in kung fu. He uses a long jab, powerful straight right hand, body kicks and unpredictable combinations to catch opponents at range, often relying on his timing and reflexes rather than conventional defensive technique. He's also a Brazilian jiu-jitsu black belt with dangerous submissions, particularly his D'Arce choke, although his takedown defense and ability to escape sustained top control have been recurring weaknesses. Holland is known for talking throughout his fights, sometimes engaging in entire conversations with opponents while exchanging punches.

**Stephen "Wonderboy" Thompson** is a two-time UFC welterweight title challenger and former undefeated kickboxer with a distinctive karate-based striking style. He fights from a wide, sideways stance, keeping his hands low and using constant footwork, stance switches and quick changes of direction to stay out of range. His offense revolves around side kicks, roundhouse kicks, straight punches and sudden blitzing combinations, often catching opponents as they move forward or attempt to close the distance. Thompson relies heavily on timing, accuracy and movement rather than trading punches, although his unconventional stance can leave him vulnerable to takedowns and prolonged wrestling exchanges.

**Charles "Chuck Buffalo" Radtke** is an aggressive welterweight who combines heavy-handed boxing with a Brazilian jiu-jitsu black belt. He likes to press forward, close the distance and throw fast, straight punches in combination, often looking to turn exchanges into a brawl where his power can make the difference. He's also a capable grappler who can threaten submissions and take the back during scrambles, although he generally prefers to fight on the feet. His willingness to trade and limited head movement leave him vulnerable to counters, particularly against faster, more accurate strikers.

**Drew Dober** has put together back-to-back stoppages of Kyle Prepolec and Michael Johnson after a difficult run of losses. He is a southpaw who is comfortable in exchanges, and the recent wins show that he remains dangerous when opponents give him the chance to build offense. Duncan adds a different concern with his takedowns and submissions. Dober needs his striking pressure to create useful exchanges without making the entries into the clinch and onto his legs too easy.

**Chris Duncan** Chris Duncan's four-fight winning streak ended when Renato Moicano submitted him in April. Before that, he submitted Bolaji Oki, Jordan Vucenic and Terrance McKinney, with a decision over Mateusz Rębecki in between. There is useful variety in that run: he can fight through difficult rounds as well as find an early choke. Dober is a dangerous opponent to get careless with on the feet, so the balance between pressure, defensive responsibility and getting to the grappling exchanges matters here.

**Jim Miller** is 43 and still finding submissions. His most recent win was the first-round guillotine against Jared Gordon in May, taking him to 22 submission victories as a professional. He has seen just about every kind of fight available at lightweight, but experience does not make McKinney's opening burst any less dangerous. The first exchanges and the first scramble are particularly important. If Miller gets a useful grip or a positional mistake to work with, the submission threat is immediate.

**Terrance McKinney** has finished all eighteen of his wins, split evenly between knockouts and submissions. His recent record has swung between quick finishes and quick defeats, with the loss to King Green following a 24-second stoppage of Kyle Nelson. He is a threat as soon as the fight begins, but Miller is an opponent who can punish a rushed grappling exchange. This matchup asks how effectively McKinney can use that explosiveness without giving a very experienced submission fighter the opening he needs.

**Macy Chiasson** has significant physical advantages here: she is seven inches taller than Mesquita and has five more inches of reach. The recent form is less encouraging, with consecutive decision losses to Ketlen Vieira, Yana Santos and Ailín Pérez. Her best opportunity is to make Mesquita work through that size and distance before she can get comfortable grappling. Letting the fight become a prolonged battle from underneath would put the matchup in a much less attractive place for Chiasson.

**Bia Mesquita** is 8-0 and has submitted all three of her UFC opponents. The most recent of those wins was a first-round armbar against Melissa Mullins, following rear-naked chokes against Montse Rendon and Irina Alekseeva. Her submission threat is well established in this short UFC run, but Chiasson presents a much bigger frame to get through. The entry and the first completed takedown are the important parts of the matchup before any of the finishing sequences can begin.

He has already shown different ways to get there: a submission over Sedriques Dumas, a split decision against Cody Brundage and a first-round stoppage of Eric McConico. Almeida replaces Baisangur Susurkaev, changing the problem in front of him. Johnson has to respect the striking threat while making use of the pace and grappling options that have helped him through his early UFC schedule.

**César Almeida** comes in after losses to Cezary Oleksiejczuk and Damian Pinas, the latter ending by knockout in July. He has five knockout wins in MMA and can punish an opponent who makes the exchanges too straightforward. Johnson is unbeaten, but has only three UFC appearances, so this is also a chance for Almeida to make his experience matter. Keeping the fight in a striking rhythm he can read is a better starting point than giving Johnson uncomplicated entries into wrestling exchanges.

**Evan Elder** has won three straight UFC fights after losing his first two. His last appearance was the decision over Gauge Young in April 2025, following wins over Genaro Valdez and Darrius Flowers. He has already shown he can win rounds and finish a fight on the ground, but this is another return after a long break. Against a newcomer with a high finishing rate, getting a useful read on the opening exchanges matters more than assuming his UFC experience will settle the fight by itself.

**Samuel Sanches** makes his UFC debut with an 11-1 record and five straight wins. Eight of his victories have come by knockout or TKO, including the first-round finish of Chasen Blair on Contender Series in September 2025. That is also the only appearance behind his UFCStats rate numbers, so the percentages need to be treated as a very small sample. Elder offers a more experienced UFC opponent and a useful test of how that finishing ability carries into a longer, more demanding fight.
