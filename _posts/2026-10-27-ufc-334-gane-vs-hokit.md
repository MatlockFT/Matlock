UFC 334: Gane vs. Hokit takes place Saturday, November 14, at Madison Square Garden in New York City. The early prelims begin at 5 p.m. ET, followed by the prelims at 7 p.m. ET and the main card at 9 p.m. ET. The card streams on Paramount+.

Ciryl Gane defends the heavyweight title against unbeaten challenger Josh Hokit in the five-round main event. The co-main pairs women's bantamweight champion Kayla Harrison with returning former two-division champion Amanda Nunes. The rest of the 12-fight card includes Caio Borralho against Yousri Belgaroui, Uroš Medić against Kevin Holland, and several matchups between established veterans and newer UFC names.

**Ciryl Gane** is at his best when he can control the distance, change the rhythm and make a heavyweight fight look a little less like a heavyweight fight. He comes in after stopping Alex Pereira in the second round in June, and his 81-inch reach gives him an eight-inch advantage here. The important question is whether he can keep Hokit working at the end of his strikes instead of letting him settle into close-range exchanges and takedowns.

**Josh Hokit** has moved quickly. He's unbeaten through ten professional fights and has already beaten Curtis Blaydes and Derrick Lewis during a 4-0 UFC run. The Lewis finish earned him this opportunity, but the Blaydes decision matters too: he has shown he can win beyond an early burst. His pace and takedown threat give Gane something to manage, although a first UFC title fight over five scheduled rounds is a different assignment.

**Kayla Harrison** has gone 3-0 in the UFC, submitting Holly Holm and Julianna Peña around a decision over Ketlen Vieira. Her game becomes especially difficult to deal with once she gets attached, starts completing takedowns and forces opponents to defend underneath her. She hasn't fought since winning the belt in June 2025, so there is a layoff on her side as well. Getting through Nunes's striking range without paying too much for the entry is the first problem to solve.

**Amanda Nunes** returns with an enormous amount of championship experience, but also more than three years away from competition. Her last appearance was the five-round win over Irene Aldana in June 2023. She has the power, a three-inch reach advantage and a submission game that makes her more than a stand-up threat. The uncertainty is how much of that timing and physical sharpness carries into a return against a champion whose strongest work happens in the clinch and on the mat.

**Luis Gurule** has had a rough start in the UFC, going 1-4 against a schedule that has included Ode Osbourne, Jesus Aguilar, Alden Coria and Rei Tsuruya. He did get a decision over Daniel Barez in May before Tsuruya submitted him later that month. He is willing to work and exchange, but the amount of offense coming back at him is a concern. Against a longer opponent with finishing momentum, he needs a fight he can manage rather than an open-ended race to trade shots.

**Bilal Hasan** is unbeaten as a professional and has finished each of his last five opponents by strikes. His UFC debut ended with a second-round stoppage of Nilson Rojas, shortly after his Contender Series win over Mridul Saikia. He has a six-inch reach advantage over Gurule and has been producing plenty of offense. The caution is the size of the sample: one UFC appearance and a contract-winning fight are still a small foundation for judging how he will handle a longer, more demanding night.

**Caio Borralho** rebounded from the Nassourdine Imavov loss by beating Reinier de Ridder in March. He has eight UFC wins, can work through longer fights and has enough grappling to give a dangerous striker several things to think about. He is five inches shorter than Belgaroui and gives up four inches of reach, so sitting at the end of the taller man's offense is not an especially appealing plan. Closing distance on his terms and making position matter should be central to the matchup.

**Yousri Belgaroui** has won all three of his UFC fights by stoppage, beating Azamat Bekoev, Mansur Abdul-Malik and Djorden Santos. At 6-foot-6 with a 79-inch reach, he presents an unusual frame for a middleweight, and his recent finishing run makes the threat fairly obvious. Borralho is a more established UFC opponent with a broader game than those numbers alone can describe. Belgaroui's challenge is keeping enough space to strike without letting that length become something Borralho can tie up and control.

**Uroš Medić** has finished all fourteen of his professional wins, twelve by knockout or TKO. His last four victories have all come in the first round, most recently the quick finish of Daniel Rodriguez in August. That is a serious amount of momentum, but Holland gives him a very different target: a taller opponent with ten more inches of reach. Medić has to find his way into range without turning every entry into an opportunity for Holland to catch him coming forward.

**Kevin Holland** is coming off a decision win over Randy Brown and remains a difficult fighter to reduce to one phase. He can hurt opponents standing, work from long range and find submissions, as the anaconda choke against Vicente Luque showed. His 81-inch reach is a major feature of this matchup. The issue is using it consistently against someone who has been ending fights very quickly, rather than giving Medić a comfortable chance to turn the opening exchanges into a scramble for a finish.

**Stephen Thompson** is still a distinctive striking matchup, with the distance changes, kicks and countering approach that have defined his career. The recent results are harder to ignore: he has lost three straight, and his last fight was the split decision against Gabriel Bonfim in July 2025. He will be 43 on fight night. There is a three-inch reach advantage for him here, but keeping the fight in his preferred spaces is especially important after another lengthy stretch away.

**Charles Radtke** has won his last two fights, submitting Daniel Frunza before taking a decision over Francisco Prado in April. He has a 5-2 UFC record and has shown routes to winning both inside the distance and over three rounds. He gives up height and reach to Thompson, which makes simply following him around the outside a risky way to spend the fight. Cutting off space and making Thompson deal with pressure and possible takedowns is the more interesting side of this matchup.

**Drew Dober** has put together back-to-back stoppages of Kyle Prepolec and Michael Johnson after a difficult run of losses. He is a southpaw who is comfortable in exchanges, and the recent wins show that he remains dangerous when opponents give him the chance to build offense. Duncan adds a different concern with his takedowns and submissions. Dober needs his striking pressure to create useful exchanges without making the entries into the clinch and onto his legs too easy.

**Chris Duncan** Chris Duncan's four-fight winning streak ended when Renato Moicano submitted him in April. Before that, he submitted Bolaji Oki, Jordan Vucenic and Terrance McKinney, with a decision over Mateusz Rębecki in between. There is useful variety in that run: he can fight through difficult rounds as well as find an early choke. Dober is a dangerous opponent to get careless with on the feet, so the balance between pressure, defensive responsibility and getting to the grappling exchanges matters here.

**Jim Miller** is 43 and still finding submissions. His most recent win was the first-round guillotine against Jared Gordon in May, taking him to 22 submission victories as a professional. He has seen just about every kind of fight available at lightweight, but experience does not make McKinney's opening burst any less dangerous. The first exchanges and the first scramble are particularly important. If Miller gets a useful grip or a positional mistake to work with, the submission threat is immediate.

**Terrance McKinney** has finished all eighteen of his wins, split evenly between knockouts and submissions. His recent record has swung between quick finishes and quick defeats, with the loss to King Green following a 24-second stoppage of Kyle Nelson. He is a threat as soon as the fight begins, but Miller is an opponent who can punish a rushed grappling exchange. This matchup asks how effectively McKinney can use that explosiveness without giving a very experienced submission fighter the opening he needs.

**Macy Chiasson** has significant physical advantages here: she is seven inches taller than Mesquita and has five more inches of reach. The recent form is less encouraging, with consecutive decision losses to Ketlen Vieira, Yana Santos and Ailín Pérez. Her best opportunity is to make Mesquita work through that size and distance before she can get comfortable grappling. Letting the fight become a prolonged battle from underneath would put the matchup in a much less attractive place for Chiasson.

**Bia Mesquita** is 8-0 and has submitted all three of her UFC opponents. The most recent of those wins was a first-round armbar against Melissa Mullins, following rear-naked chokes against Montse Rendon and Irina Alekseeva. Her submission threat is well established in this short UFC run, but Chiasson presents a much bigger frame to get through. The entry and the first completed takedown are the important parts of the matchup before any of the finishing sequences can begin.

**Donte Johnson** is unbeaten through nine professional fights and has won all three of his UFC appearances. He has already shown different ways to get there: a submission over Sedriques Dumas, a split decision against Cody Brundage and a first-round stoppage of Eric McConico. Almeida replaces Baisangur Susurkaev, changing the problem in front of him. Johnson has to respect the striking threat while making use of the pace and grappling options that have helped him through his early UFC schedule.

**César Almeida** comes in after losses to Cezary Oleksiejczuk and Damian Pinas, the latter ending by knockout in July. He has five knockout wins in MMA and can punish an opponent who makes the exchanges too straightforward. Johnson is unbeaten, but has only three UFC appearances, so this is also a chance for Almeida to make his experience matter. Keeping the fight in a striking rhythm he can read is a better starting point than giving Johnson uncomplicated entries into wrestling exchanges.

**Jared Gordon** has lost his last two fights, getting stopped by Rafa García and then submitted by Jim Miller. His most recent win was the first-round knockout of Thiago Moisés. Costa has a height and reach advantage here, so Gordon's ability to close that gap without taking unnecessary damage is an important part of the fight.

**Evan Elder** has won three straight UFC fights after losing his first two. His last appearance was the decision over Gauge Young in April 2025, following wins over Genaro Valdez and Darrius Flowers. He has already shown he can win rounds and finish a fight on the ground, but this is another return after a long break. Against a newcomer with a high finishing rate, getting a useful read on the opening exchanges matters more than assuming his UFC experience will settle the fight by itself.

**Samuel Sanches** makes his UFC debut with an 11-1 record and five straight wins. Eight of his victories have come by knockout or TKO, including the first-round finish of Chasen Blair on Contender Series in September 2025. That is also the only appearance behind his UFCStats rate numbers, so the percentages need to be treated as a very small sample. Elder offers a more experienced UFC opponent and a useful test of how that finishing ability carries into a longer, more demanding fight.
