## Alexander Volkanovski vs. Movsar Evloev

[HTML VISUAL · Alexander Volkanovski · #c121e3f7]
**Alexander Volkanovski** is still one of the most complete fighters in MMA. He builds everything off feints, stance changes and angle shifts, mixes his boxing with hard body and leg work, and has the wrestling and cardio to keep changing the fight over five rounds. He has now beaten Diego Lopes twice since the back-to-back knockout losses to Islam Makhachev and Ilia Topuria.

**Movsar Evloev** is an undefeated chain wrestler who is very good at turning one failed shot into the next position. He pressures opponents toward the fence, keeps working through scrambles and has become much more comfortable boxing his way into those exchanges. He is 10-0 in the UFC and does not need a finish to control long stretches of a fight.

## Petr Yan vs. Merab Dvalishvili

[HTML VISUAL · Petr Yan · #c0b8e34a]
**Petr Yan** is a switch-stance boxer who gets better as he reads the fight. He is excellent at layering combinations, attacking the body and making small defensive adjustments from round to round. In the second Merab fight he shut down almost all of the wrestling and reclaimed the title, which makes the trilogy a completely different problem than the first meeting.

**Merab Dvalishvili** wins fights by making opponents solve the same exhausting problem over and over again. He chains takedowns, clinch entries and mat returns together at a ridiculous pace and is willing to shoot again immediately after being stuffed. His 14-fight unbeaten run ended against Yan in the rematch, where Yan defended 27 of 29 takedown attempts.

## Lone'er Kavanagh vs. Ramazan Temirov

[HTML VISUAL · Lone&#39;er Kavanagh · #c66bb82f]
**Lone'er Kavanagh** is a fast, technical kickboxer who likes to change stance, create angles and counter as opponents step into range. The win over Brandon Moreno showed he can stay composed and effective over five rounds, while the Royval loss was another reminder that the scramble and submission layer is still dangerous territory for him.

**Ramazan Temirov** is a compact pressure striker with real first-round power. He throws hard in combination, closes distance quickly and has started his UFC run 3-0, most recently knocking out Steve Erceg in the first round. The 100% takedown defense looks great, but it is still a pretty small UFC sample.

## Alexander Volkov vs. Rizvan Kuniev

[HTML VISUAL · Alexander Volkov · #1e784fed]
**Alexander Volkov** is a rangy, high-volume heavyweight who uses his jab, front kicks and body attacks better than most big men. His clinch work and top game have become much more useful as he has gotten older, and he has put together a strong run by making opponents deal with his size for the full fight.

**Rizvan Kuniev** is a strong, compact heavyweight who pressures well, works the body and is difficult to wrestle cleanly. He lost a close split decision to Curtis Blaydes in his UFC debut, then came back with a decision over Jailton Almeida and a third-round stoppage of Tyrell Fortune.

## Aaron Pico vs. Losene Keita

[HTML VISUAL · Aaron Pico · #efaf17b0]
**Aaron Pico** has an elite wrestling background but has spent most of his career trying to turn that athleticism into violence on the feet. He is explosive to the body and head, changes levels hard and can overwhelm people quickly. After getting knocked out by Lerone Murphy in his UFC debut, he came back with a much more disciplined, wrestling-heavy win over Patricio Pitbull.

**Losene Keita** is an explosive counter striker with fast feet and serious power. He lost a close split decision to Nathaniel Wood in his UFC debut, then came back and starched Muhammad Naimov in the first round. The defensive wrestling numbers look strong so far, but there is barely any UFC grappling sample behind them.

[HTML VISUAL · Aaron Pico vs. Losene Keita · Stats · #005d8f8c]

## Azamat Murzakanov vs. Dominick Reyes

[HTML VISUAL · Azamat Murzakanov · #e702c401]
**Azamat Murzakanov** is a compact southpaw with a lot of power and a good feel for countering as opponents enter. He attacks the body well and was 6-0 in the UFC before Paulo Costa handed him his first career loss. Even at 37, his combination of power and takedown defense makes him a difficult matchup.

**Dominick Reyes** is a long southpaw power striker who does his best work with the straight left and intercepting counters. He has rebuilt himself after the ugly losing streak and has won four of his last five, although the Carlos Ulberg loss showed he can still get caught clean when the pocket gets chaotic.

[HTML VISUAL · Azamat Murzakanov vs. Dominick Reyes · Stats · #7d3c3de9]

## Nikita Krylov vs. Abdul Rakhman Yakhyaev

[HTML VISUAL · Nikita Krylov · #2aee5fe3]
**Nikita Krylov** is basically built for chaos. He can hurt people standing, wrestle aggressively and jump on submissions from positions where most fighters would slow down. Twenty-nine of his 31 wins are finishes, but the same aggression and defensive gaps have made his own results pretty volatile.

**Abdul Rakhman Yakhyaev** is an undefeated wrecking ball who has not needed much time to get through anybody lately. He can wrestle, submit people and has enough first-exchange power to end a fight before it settles in. His UFCStats rates are absurd right now, but they are built on an extremely small sample and should be read that way.

[HTML VISUAL · Nikita Krylov vs. Abdul Rakhman Yakhyaev · Stats · #e993d421]

## Abus Magomedov vs. Cam Rowston

[HTML VISUAL · Abus Magomedov · #1bd0e1a0]
**Abus Magomedov** is a big, rangy middleweight who likes front kicks, long straight attacks and fighting behind his length. He has also leaned into his wrestling and submission game more consistently lately. The concern has always been what happens to his pace when an opponent survives the early offense and keeps making him work.

**Cam Rowston** is a long City Kickboxing middleweight with clean straight striking, knees and front kicks, plus a legit submission game when the fight gets messy. He is 3-0 in the UFC with two stoppages, although the earlier Contender Series loss to Torrez Finney is still useful tape whenever he faces a strong wrestling matchup.

[HTML VISUAL · Abus Magomedov vs. Cam Rowston · Stats · #0feedbcc]

## Grant Dawson vs. Nurullo Aliev

[HTML VISUAL · Grant Dawson · #69c344f0]
**Grant Dawson** is a pressure grappler who wants to chain takedowns, take the back and keep opponents carrying his weight. His striking is functional enough to get him into the wrestling, but he can be hurt badly before the grappling gets going. He rebounded from the Manuel Torres knockout by submitting Mateusz Rebecki.

**Nurullo Aliev** is an undefeated positional wrestler who is perfectly comfortable winning long stretches with fence work, takedowns and top control. He is patient and low risk, which is why all four of his UFC wins have gone to decision. This is a much more interesting wrestling matchup than most of the fights he has had so far.

[HTML VISUAL · Grant Dawson vs. Nurullo Aliev · Stats · #3e4291bd]

## Colby Thicknesse vs. Asaf Chopurov

[HTML VISUAL · Colby Thicknesse · #597a43e7]
**Colby Thicknesse** is a Freestyle MMA bantamweight with a wrestling and jiu-jitsu base who is active in scrambles and always looking to improve position. He lost his short-notice UFC debut to Aleksandre Topuria, then settled in with consecutive decision wins over Josias Musasa and Vince Morales.

**Asaf Chopurov** is an undefeated Sambo 34 prospect with 10 finishes in 11 wins and a pretty even split between knockouts and submissions. He is making his UFC debut here, so there is no UFCStats sample to pretend is meaningful yet.

[HTML VISUAL · Colby Thicknesse vs. Asaf Chopurov · Stats · #ffa03967]

## Jacob Malkoun vs. Abubakar Vagaev

[HTML VISUAL · Jacob Malkoun · #3490ef78]
**Jacob Malkoun** is an Australian grinder who builds his game around a very high wrestling pace and top control. His pressure striking has improved enough to make the entries less obvious, and he comes in off back-to-back decisions over Torrez Finney and Gerald Meerschaert.

**Abubakar Vagaev** is a veteran, wrestling-heavy middleweight who has spent most of his career winning rounds through control. Twenty of his 25 wins have gone to decision, so he is not usually looking to force a finish. His UFC debut was a solid three-round win over Saygid Izagakhmaev.

[HTML VISUAL · Jacob Malkoun vs. Abubakar Vagaev · Stats · #ac24c072]

## Bolaji Oki vs. Daniel Santos

[HTML VISUAL · Bolaji Oki · #07a953f1]
**Bolaji Oki** is a high-output striker who likes to put combinations together and work the body, but he also gives opponents a lot of chances to fire back. The pace makes him fun, but he is coming in 1-3 over his last four and has been finished in three of his four career losses.

**Daniel Santos** is a Chute Boxe pressure fighter who mixes spinning attacks, kicks and knees with a useful wrestling game. He is comfortable making fights ugly and high volume, but that also means he absorbs plenty of offense. His four-fight UFC winning streak ended against Dooho Choi in May.

## Kody Steele vs. Noah Gugnon

[HTML VISUAL · Kody Steele · #a5a08e1c]
**Kody Steele** is a high-level grappler with a dangerous leg-lock game and enough confidence to attack submissions immediately. The striking numbers are still rough and he absorbed a lot in his UFC debut loss, but he bounced back by heel hooking Dom Mar Fan in the first round.

**Noah Gugnon** is a young finisher out of French/Bulgarian Top Team and all 10 of his wins have ended inside the distance. His UFC debut lasted one round before he submitted Milos Janicic with a rear-naked choke. The per-minute and submission rates come from just 1:21 of UFC action, so they're a tiny sample rather than a stable statistical profile.

## Iasmin Lucindo vs. Nicolle Caliari

[HTML VISUAL · Iasmin Lucindo · #2724ae99]
**Iasmin Lucindo** is a physical, well-rounded strawweight who can pressure on the feet and turn that pressure into fence wrestling and top control. She is 5-2 in the UFC and has already beaten several experienced names, but she will be returning from a long layoff by the time this fight happens.

**Nicolle Caliari** is an aggressive finisher with a karate and Muay Thai background plus a dangerous submission game. All nine of her career wins are stoppages, and her first UFC win came when she submitted Shauna Bannon with an arm-triangle in the third round. 5.88 is a lot of absorbed significant strikes per minute. 

[HTML VISUAL · Iasmin Lucindo vs. Nicolle Caliari · Stats · #8af12133]

Caliari is a very frustrating fighter to watch. She's had a tumultuous relationship with the sport of MMA, to say the least. It seems she has dialed in her weight issues after getting her implants removed, now fighting at strawweight where she should be. More than that, her game hasn't really developed significantly since her pro debut. 

There are very brief moments in fights where it seems like she has a grip on what she wants to do in the striking before ultimately abandoning it to throw down and crash into a clinch. She has power in her right hand, her kicks aren't bad too either. But she cannot adapt at range with someone who gets the better of her in the exchanges and will eventually blitz, cause a bit of chaos, clinch and hopefully she finds something else after that. Any fighter that can fight off the back foot or counter and has any type of ability to break from the clinch will beat Caliari. She's a very emotional fighter and I would be willing to bet a nightmare to coach. That being said, she seems to have made minor improvements to her throws and positions on the mat but I think that's mostly because she is fighting at the appropriate weight class. She's also tough and can take an asswhooping like she did against Judice. Here's a compilation of Caliari throughout the years. The good, the bad, and the ugly. 

Iasmin Lucindo is a top heavy BJJ player who has really developed her striking over the years, primarily landing heavy counters. She also has an affinity for jumping knees and spinning shit. Her footwork still needs some work as she's borderline Cat Zingano from the waist down but has certinitely improved. She has some natural power which was likely aided by some of them supplements she popped for. Her fight IQ is pretty good considering the landscape and I believe is what really got her the decision over Marina Rodriguez who couldn't get out of her own way. So if you scored it for Rodriguez, Lucindo is 3-2 in her last five. She really doesn't seem to mind and might even prefer her opponent to charge forward so she can land her clubbing counters. She's competent enough in the clinch to tie up and use her strength to hold position until she can find a way to get it to the ground where she'll continue to have a very patient, top heavy BJJ game. She does not mind holding a position for minutes on end but as soon as her opponent m akes a move on bottom, she'll take that opportunity to advance her own position either for something more dominant, to find openings for punches, or for a submission. 

This is good matchmaking because both these girls have been fighting these rangy, spindly strikers.
