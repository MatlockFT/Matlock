When I heard Scotty Cokes was coming back to start a new MMA promotion I though, that's cool but... why?

Since I'm very familiar with Scott's background and the promotions he's been apart of, I felt I had a good idea of what he would come up with. 

Tournaments and Super Fights. 

So I had to do a memory refresh and read some old Scott Coker interviews and the first one I read summed everything up perfectly. 

> We are not going to be that company that goes out and hires 100 people and grows haphazardly or be so top heavy that just can’t make money. We are going to grow the business organically and as needed. So far I am very happy with the business model that has been carried out. Hopefully our over 20 years of experience will kick-in during the times when we need it.

> We would like to hearken back to the old PRIDE days when you used to see a lot of big fights in one night. Not just a headliner and four “okay” fights. That is what we want to get back to. When a fan can come in, pay as little as $30 to get into the stadium, and see five superstar fights. I think that everyone would like to see that.

You would think this is a quote from Scott Coker talking about Ki MMA but its not. 
This is Scott Coker talking to Brian Oswald of MMAMania.com in 2009 about Strikeforce. 

This is only one interview but there are many others of him talking about Pride and even tournaments. Its no surprise he landed on the tournament format with regular bouts and super fights surrounding it all hosted inside of a ring. 

However, what is very surprising is when they announced Ki MMA they didn't answer some of the most major questions:

- Who is fighting?
- When are they fighting?
- Where are they fighting?

Not only did they not announce a single fighter signing but they flat out said they haven't signed anymore yet. They also are dancing around the questions about TV/streaming distribution so we haven't a single clue about where we can watch it. They said they want their first event to be in the San Jose and in Q1 of 2027 but there is nothing concrete. 

I can't help but wonder why they event held an event to announce Ki MMA in the first place. Are they a little behind in their goals but felt confident enough in what's in the works to proceed with it anyway? 

The event had a lot of legends and fighters that Scott Coker had built up through Strikeforce or had good relationships at the event but that was moreso to show everyone the work Scott has put in over the years. He's signaling that Ki MMA is going to be good because look at what I did before. That's not a bad thing but at the same time, none of them are signed with Ki and no fights with any of them were announced. 

Personally, I am rooting for Ki. I like the ring and was kind of surprised by how negative the fan reaction was to it. A lot of groaning about resets. I guess wrestling against the cage is more accepted these days. Then I was also surprised just how many people flat out don't want another MMA promotion. UFC loyalists. I don't necessarily hate them for that take either because the UFC is such a juggernaut with not only their massive roster of mostly all the best fighters, they also have their own feeder leagues through The Ultimate Fighter, Dana White's Contender Series, and Road To UFC. PFL also has a stable of fighters tied up and then there is RIZIN in Japan. So who is going to fight? All we know right now is Featherweight prospects with a ballpark of 10 fights and about 20 something years old and we as fans have to sit through what will be a year long tournament in 4 different countries to see if we like any of them. 

Ki MMA wants to do better storytelling... well they are really, REALLY setting themselves up for a massive challenge and the only hint of storytelling we've seen so far has all been extremely bad AI videos of AI fighters doing AI MMA in an AI ring... and as I said earlier, no distribution partner. 

I'd grade the Ki MMA announcement event a C-. They failed to answer the big questions. For Scott Coker and people in the business, they'd probably rate it much higher because it really was just a networking opportunity for everyone. What sucks is that Ariel Helwani was there, arrived late, talked with a lot of people, and didn't zero boots on the ground journalism. The way he described it just sounded like a high school reunion. 

Scott's new promotion really has me asking: What is the MMA ecosystem lacking?

When I look across the landscape, is there really anything I'm desiring? I think about card quality, I think about past rivalries and getting super excited before a fight, I think about the heavyweight division, and I guess I do think about storytelling. 

I had mentioned not long ago that I felt the story telling in MMA has really been lacking and since then, I haven't really heard that talked about until Rich Chou mentioned it during the Ki press event. He didn't go into much detail but I did find that interesting. 

When I think of storytelling in MMA, I think about the promotion learning about a fighter and their unique and presenting it in interesting ways. That could include tons of pre-fight video packages so its certainly an investment of time and money. The even more important and difficult part is actually finding a fighter who has something interesting to say. If you're not aware, it is extremely difficult to find a fighter who is actually interesting, who has charisma, who isn't just a carbon copy of the fighter to their left or right. A lot of fighters love a day to day, structured routine which is why embedded became stale so quickly. Embedded used to be Dana White's vlog and they'd follow Dana White around and his interactions with fighters. If Dana White brought back those vlogs in 2026, it would do tremendous numbers and it would be extremely clippable.

So how can Ki MMA, Scott Coker, and Rich Chou deliver on their want to improve storytelling? Finding a fighter with that "it-factor" is highly unlikely early on. I'll vomit if they do another embedded series because every fight promotion has stolen that idea who have the resources. I think about things like RIZIN's pre-fight video packages for each fighter which are phenomenal. Watching Shinya Aoki riding his bike around town, meeting fans, then complaining about it was great. It's just different enough to be interesting. Sitting a fighter down and asking them stupid questions only to get stupid generic answers back for a small video promo is always just a waste of everyone's time.

I'm not sure how they can do it but they need to touch on the live streaming and/or vlog-style video formats because those tend to be longer in length, deliver more natural and real interactions, and can be clipped for short-form content. Scott Coker is on a quest to find a star but I don't think he's got the charisma to lead a vlog or live stream but.. maybe that could work in his favor? Maybe they can have a face that does it for him like Josh Barnett or someone with personality to interface with fighters and take people backstage during fight nights. 

They could also just take a camera backstage after fights while fighters do their post-fight medicals and just leave the camera rolling. UFC used to do that with the Dana White vlogs, too. Some of the most amazing content, in my opinion, are those interactions in the bowels of the arena between fighters after they've fought. These things bring soul and the human element back into MMA. UFC, since 2016-ish, has focused more and more on getting a knockout and getting it quick. It doesn't matter if its two geeks off the street, as long as they throw down and someone gets a highlight worthy finish. This push for knockouts at all costs, in my opinion, has completely devalued the nuances of what it takes to be a skilled MMA fighter. Look no further than DWCS. I see waves of new fans tune in to bet, yell and scream and praise two donks who will never touch gold in the UFC but because they left it all in the cage and got out of there in under five minutes, its the best fight they've ever seen. Fights that go to decision are now automatically assumed to be terrible fights. I'd like to think this an insignificant portion of the fanbase but I really don't think it is. Sure, they'll probably stop watching MMA in 3-5 years but there is always another crop of fair-weather fans ready to replace them

Aside from the bells and whistles, as Dana White likes to call it, I think one of the major issues with MMA today are the rules and reffing. If Scott Coker could somehow separate himself from other organizations by having slightly different rules and also how they're enforced could be massive. I think about point deductions and yellow cards. I think about how refs can be used to stop stalling. They could rethink how a fight is scored. They could do open scoring. There are just a whole list of things they could possibly experiment with especially if an athletic commission is open to it. Scott Coker has a history in California and likely a good relationship with Andy Foster and CSAC. In my opinion, slight modifications to the rules could be what truly separates them from the UFC and other MMA promotions. 

However, without this, we may unfortunately be subject to a tournament style MMA organization that may become tough to follow or flat out not interesting to stick with and veteran-slop "super fights".
