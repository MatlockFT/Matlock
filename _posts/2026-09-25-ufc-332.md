**Natalia Silva** is a fast, movement-heavy striker with a taekwondo base who does a great job changing angles, mixing kicks and staying hard to read. She’s very comfortable fighting at range, but she’s also a legit submission threat if the fight hits the mat. She’s won 14 straight and is coming off wins over Rose Namajunas, Alexa Grasso and Jessica Andrade.

**Wang Cong** is a polished kickboxer and sanda fighter with very sharp hands, good timing and a pretty composed counter-striking game. She’s accurate, physically strong and usually does her best work when she can keep things technical on the feet. She’s won four straight since her only career loss, most recently beating Tracy Cortez.

**Deiveson Figueiredo** is a compact orthodox fighter with big power, sharp counters and a dangerous submission game. He’s still very explosive in short bursts and does a good job mixing hard low kicks with sudden level changes, but at bantamweight he’s been a little more measured than he was at 125. He’s coming off back-to-back losses to Umar Nurmagomedov and Song Yadong.

**Payton Talbott** is a tall, fluid switch-stance striker who likes to build combinations as the fight goes on. He throws a lot of volume, works well off straight punches and is dangerous when he starts getting opponents backed up, while still being comfortable scrambling or attacking on the ground. He’s rebounded from the Barcelos loss with decision wins over Felipe Lima and Henry Cejudo.

**Esteban Ribovics** is an aggressive orthodox striker who’s much more comfortable turning fights into high-paced exchanges. He throws hard combinations, attacks the body well and will switch stance during exchanges to create different angles. He’s coming off a big second-round TKO win over Edson Barboza and has become one of the more dangerous action fighters at lightweight.

**Roberto Soldic** is a heavy-handed southpaw who likes to pressure behind the left hand and left kick. He’s patient until he sees an opening, then he explodes, and most of his wins have come by knockout. He’s making his UFC debut after flattening Dagi Arslanaliev with a first-round left hook in his last fight.

**Khaos Williams** is an explosive orthodox striker with serious one-shot power, especially when he has room to counter. He can be a little low-volume at times, but when he commits, his speed and power make him dangerous immediately. He snapped a two-fight skid in May with a first-round TKO of Nikolay Veretennikov.

**Ateba Gautier** is a huge, switch-stance middleweight with an 81-inch reach and serious knockout power. He likes to work at range, explode forward with straight punches and knees, and he’s especially dangerous once he has someone hurt. Four of his five UFC wins have come by knockout, including his last fight against Ozzy Diaz.

**Roman Kopylov** is a southpaw kickboxer who’s very comfortable fighting at distance and building offense behind his kicks. He throws well to the body, has a sharp left hand and tends to get more dangerous once he settles into the rhythm of a fight. He’s historically been a big finisher, with 12 of his 15 wins coming by knockout, and he most recently beat Marco Tulio by decision.

**Imanol Rodriguez** is a compact, explosive flyweight with serious finishing power. He likes to pressure, sit down on his punches and throw hard in combination, and he’s finished all seven of his pro wins — six by knockout. He’s 7-0 and coming off a second-round TKO of Kevin Borjas. On The Ultimate Fighter, he does carry a split decision loss to Joseph Morales and a first round KO over Arshiyan Memon.

**Alden Coria** is a more seasoned, well-rounded flyweight who mixes boxing, wrestling and submissions pretty comfortably. He’s defensively responsible on the feet, doesn’t absorb much clean offense and has shown he can either grind out rounds or find a finish late. He’s won three straight in the UFC, including a third-round stoppage of Alessandro Costa.

**Damian Pinas** is an orthodox power puncher with a long reach and a very aggressive finishing style. He works well behind the one-two, carries serious power in the right hand and is especially dangerous once he gets someone backing up. He’s 10-1 with nine knockouts and has finished both of his UFC fights in the first round.

**Andrey Pulyaev** is a tall southpaw who prefers to strike at range and uses his length, body kicks and straight left well. He’s more measured than Pinas and generally looks to pick shots rather than force exchanges, but his grappling defense has been a weak point. He’s coming off losses to Ateba Gautier and Nursulton Ruziboev.

**Marcus McGhee** is an orthodox striker with sharp boxing, good power and a nice feel for countering while still pushing the pace. He does most of his work at distance and has quietly become very hard to wrestle, defending about 90% of takedowns in the UFC. He bounced back from the Petr Yan loss with a decision win over John Yannis.

Romero is dangerous and has finished six of his seven wins, so he’s not some easy late replacement.

He’s explosive, throws hard in short combinations and is very comfortable crashing into the clinch or turning that pressure into takedowns. He’s 8-0 with seven finishes and submitted Terrance Chatman in his UFC debut.

This is a glorified DWCS Series fight that really should not be taking place on a UFC card. These guys are so early into their careers. But the UFC is desperate for heavyweights, and not too often do they get a shredded athlete like Wint at heavyweight, so... I guess they're going to take advantage early.

I'll be kind. Armand has fought some pretty low-level competition, and against that level he's looked OK. He's shown decent enough footwork, throws shots to the body, and has a basic one-two that works for him. A lot of the time, though, he ends up closing the distance too aggressively and running himself into clinch exchanges along the fence.

Wint is kind of the same type of fighter, but with way worse striking and footwork. However, he is a shredded athlete with some high school wrestling credentials in his back pocket, so getting into those wrestling exchanges isn't necessarily a bad thing for Wint.

If Armand can't establish the jab early and keep Wint at a comfortable distance, I think Wint is just going to crash his way into the clinch. From there, he'll pummel for position, look to get Armand down, and take whatever presents itself on the ground, whether that's ground-and-pound or a submission. Armand had trouble dealing with that kind of physicality against Cameron Graham (9-26), who is a bad but experienced fighter, and Graham even managed to toss him on his ass at one point. Check the video above.

I don't find Wint very impressive at all, and I think it's only a matter of time before someone head-kicks his head into the nosebleeds, but I don't think it's going to be Armand unless Armand has vastly improved his takedown defense. Wint has struggled against fighters like Armand before, like Omar El-Sahlah, who took him to a split decision. But Omar had slightly better striking and definitely a better ability to defend the takedown.

We'll see what they can do here. They're both still early in their careers, but they're already 30, so you'd hope to see some noticeable improvements from what they've shown so far. Normally, I'd lean toward Wint getting a finish in the first round, and that's still very possible. But with both guys being relatively inexperienced and fighting at roughly 4,200 feet above sea level, there's also a decent chance this turns into a three-round slog.

**Johnny Walker** is a long, explosive heavyweight who fights pretty loose and unpredictably. He likes kicks, knees and sudden bursts from range, and he still carries serious finishing power when he decides to open up. He’s also become a little more patient recently instead of constantly forcing chaos. He most recently lost a close split decision to Dominick Reyes.

**Mick Parkin** is a more traditional heavyweight who fights behind steady boxing, pressure and a solid wrestling game. He’s fairly composed, throws good volume for the division and is comfortable mixing takedowns in rather than relying entirely on power. His first professional loss came by decision to Marcin Tybura in his last fight.

**Rafael dos Anjos** is a southpaw veteran with a pressure-heavy, well-rounded game. He likes to work behind hard body kicks and boxing, then mix in clinch control and takedowns when opponents give him the opportunity. He’s returning after nearly two years out following the knee injury against Geoff Neal.

**Alexander Hernandez** is an explosive orthodox striker who likes to burst into range with fast combinations and big power shots. He can wrestle too, but he’s generally at his best when he can stay aggressive and dictate the pace on the feet. He’s coming off a decision win over Rafa Garcia after previously stopping Diego Ferreira and Chase Hooper.

Whitehead is tough and has a legit wrestling/BJJ background, but this is a massive jump. He’s making his UFC debut just 13 days after his last fight, and now he gets an undefeated Oklahoma State wrestler in Smith who has finished all three of his UFC fights. Neither guy trains at real altitude, so I don’t see much of an edge there.

I think Smith is just too physical and too good of a wrestler. Whitehead may hang around for a little bit, but once Smith starts forcing grappling exchanges and letting his hands go off them, I think he overwhelms him.
