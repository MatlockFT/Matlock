UFC Vegas 124 takes place Saturday, November 7, at Meta APEX in Las Vegas. The prelims begin at 5 p.m. ET (4 p.m. CT), followed by the main card at 8 p.m. ET (7 p.m. CT). The full card streams on Paramount+.

Waldo Cortes-Acosta meets Johnny Walker in a short-notice heavyweight main event after Sean Brady withdrew from his scheduled fight with Gabriel Bonfim. The Austin Bashi–Lucas Brennan bout also changed, with Carlos Calderon taking Brennan's place on short notice. Walker is coming off his first heavyweight appearance, while Cortes-Acosta gets an opportunity to headline. Tatiana Suarez and Virna Jandiroba meet in the women's strawweight co-main event.

**Waldo Cortes-Acosta** is a heavyweight boxer who relies on combinations, timing and a steady pace rather than hunting a single knockout punch. His offense is built around working from striking range and keeping opponents occupied with volume.

**Johnny Walker** is an unpredictable, long-range striker whose offense features kicks, knees and explosive bursts from awkward angles. His first heavyweight appearance has given him a different physical matchup from the light heavyweight competition he faced for most of his UFC career.

**Billy Elekana** is a southpaw light heavyweight who turned professional in 2021 and competed for LFA before winning decisions over Tristan Overvig and Chuck Campbell in the PFL in 2023. He stopped Jureall Simmons by head-kick-and-punches TKO at LXF 18 in July 2024. His UFC debut ended in a second-round guillotine loss to Bogdan Guskov in January 2025. Since then, Elekana has outpointed Ibo Aslan and submitted Kevin Christian and Junior Tafa with rear-naked chokes. His recent success has come from taking advantage of scrambles and finding the back.

**Lucas Fernando** began his professional career in 2017 after an extensive kickboxing background. He won the LFA middleweight title by knocking out Jansey Silva with a body punch in March 2023, then lost decisions to Cesar Almeida on Dana White's Contender Series and Azamat Bekoev in an LFA title unification bout. He rebounded with decisions over Joao Paulo Silva Santos and Pedro Oliveira before moving to light heavyweight, where he finished Phillip Latu and Leon Soares in LFA title bouts. Fernando made his UFC debut in August 2026 and stopped Rafael Tobias with a knee to the body in Round 3. His striking accuracy and experience across two weight classes stand out, but his UFC sample remains small.

**Mitch Raposo** turned professional in 2019 after compiling a reported 6-0 amateur record. He was Team Volkanovski's first overall pick on The Ultimate Fighter 29 in 2021, losing an exhibition decision to Liudvik Sholinian. In October 2021, Jake Hadley submitted him by rear-naked choke on Dana White's Contender Series. Raposo returned to regional promotions and defeated Jay Viola, Flavio Carvalho and Tyler Smythe before stopping Justin Valentin in Round 4 at Cage Titans 63 in January 2024. He debuted in the UFC on short notice at UFC 302, losing a split decision to Andre Lima, followed by another split-decision loss to Sumudaerji at UFC 314. He has since won decisions against Azat Maksum at UFC 321 and Allan Nascimento in June 2026. Raposo mixes movement and wrestling, but his relatively low significant-strike output and mixed takedown success present questions against an aggressive finisher.

**Marcos Degli** turned professional in 2021 and lost three of his first five fights before building a 13-fight winning streak. He competed throughout the Brazilian regional circuit, including Jungle Fight and LFA, and stopped Ignacio Fernandez in Round 1 at LFA 187 in July 2024. He won the interim LFA flyweight title by finishing Lincon Santos in Round 2 at LFA 201 in February 2025, then defended against Matheus Severino with a third-round TKO at LFA 212 and Luis Aguiar with a first-round body-shot TKO at LFA 225 in January 2026. Degli secured his UFC contract on September 22, 2026, submitting Paris Moran by rear-naked choke at 4:46 of Round 2 on Dana White's Contender Series. He enters his official UFC debut with nine KO/TKO wins, five submissions and a 93% career finishing rate. His displayed striking and grappling averages are derived from only the Moran fight.

**Keiichiro Nakamura** turned professional in April 2022, losing his debut to Yu Karino by arm-triangle choke in Round 3. He has since won eight consecutive professional fights. His early run included knockout victories over Tomomitsu Hinuma, Masaya Ishizuka, Taisei Otsuki and Shun Murata, followed by a first-round knockout of Yusuke Kagiyama at Poundout 1 in October 2024. He entered Road to UFC Season 4 in 2025, stopping Eoh Jin Park with a knee in the second round, outpointing Kaiwen Li in the semifinals, and finishing Sebastian Szalay with a knee and follow-up punches at 3:48 of the third round of the January 2026 tournament final held on UFC 325. The EXFIGHT southpaw has seven knockout wins in eight career victories and has yet to win by submission or lose a decision. His listed UFC record is 1-0 when the UFC 325 final is counted; the promotion's separate Road to UFC classification produces a 0-0 pre-debut record.

**Ollie Schmid** is a City Kickboxing featherweight whose martial arts background includes a black belt in taekwondo and a reported 8-3 amateur MMA record. He turned professional in May 2023 with a technical submission loss to Harry Webb, then knocked out Dane Edge in May 2024 before suffering a third-round TKO loss to Dimps Gillies the following month. Schmid responded with a unanimous decision over Saynarvat Srisawat in March 2025, a first-round knockout of Ford Gerbes that July and a 15-second spinning-backfist knockout of Jeffrey Mesa in March 2026. He entered the UFC against Marwan Rahiki on May 2, 2026, but was stopped by punches at 2:47 of the first round. All three of his professional knockout wins came in Round 1. His UFC statistical sample remains limited to that single 2:47 loss, making his striking and grappling rates especially volatile.
