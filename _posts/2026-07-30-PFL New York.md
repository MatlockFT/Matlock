# NAMES TO WATCH AT PFL NEW YORK

## Magomedov Heads a Strong List of Rising Talent; Colgan Faces His Supreme Test

THE Professional Fighters League moves into New York on Friday, July 31, with nine bouts on the program and several ambitious young battlers seeking a place among the promotion’s top brass.

The preliminary contests get under way at 3 p.m., Central time, with the main card following at 6. The nine-bout program includes a late light-heavyweight pairing between unbeaten Moustapha Diakhaté and Darryl Walker.

Usman Nurmagomedov’s lightweight championship defense against Archie Colgan tops the bill, but the supporting card contains several names well worth filing away for future reference.

Some are prospects in the strict sense. Others have already reached contender or championship rank. All have something important to prove in New York.

## AMRU MAGOMEDOV

### The Best Prospect on the Bill

AMRU MAGOMEDOV, unbeaten in ten starts, appears to be the best orthodox prospect on the program.

The 27-year-old lightweight has disposed of eight opponents inside the distance and made a smashing PFL debut by halting Kolton Englund in only 37 seconds. That brief engagement did not tell us everything about Magomedov, but it told us plenty about his intentions.

He comes forward, closes the distance rapidly and applies the familiar pressure-wrestling attack associated with the Nurmagomedov camp. Magomedov, however, is no mere takedown specialist. He handles himself capably on his feet, strikes with authority and appears comfortable carrying the attack wherever the fight may travel.

Angel Alvarez, 11-2, should offer more resistance than Englund did.

The principal question is what happens if Alvarez survives the first rush. Magomedov has looked formidable when permitted to seize command at once. New York may reveal whether he can maintain that pace, make adjustments and remain dangerous after an opponent has spoiled his original plan.

A decisive victory would mark Magomedov as genuine championship timber in the lightweight class.

## DAKOTA DITCHEVA

### A Champion With Star Possibilities

DAKOTA DITCHEVA can hardly be called a prospect any longer.

The undefeated English flyweight has won all 15 of her professional engagements, nine of them under the PFL banner, and has stopped 12 opponents. She has already captured tournament honors and defeated established international competition.

What remains to be discovered is not whether Ditcheva belongs among the leading PFL fighters. She does.

The question is how far her combination of ability, personality and crowd appeal can carry her.

Ditcheva is tall for the weight, quick with her hands and particularly effective when opponents retreat in straight lines. She strikes in combinations, attacks the body and has shown the poise of a much more experienced campaigner.

Denise Kielholtz is no novice. She has an extensive background in kickboxing and judo and possesses enough experience to punish carelessness. Nevertheless, the match appears favorable to Ditcheva, who should hold the advantages in range, speed and offensive variety.

The condition of Ditcheva’s hands bears watching. Injuries have interrupted her activity and kept her from maintaining the momentum created by her championship run.

Should she return in top form, Ditcheva may be not only the leading woman in the PFL ranks but one of the organization’s most valuable attractions.

## ARCHIE COLGAN

### The Night’s Biggest Opportunity

NO fighter on the card has more to gain than Archie Colgan.

Colgan enters the championship contest unbeaten in 13 professional bouts. A former collegiate wrestler, he has advanced behind physical strength, steady pressure and the ability to force opponents into exhausting grappling exchanges.

Those weapons have brought him to Usman Nurmagomedov.

Now we shall learn whether they can carry him any farther.

Nurmagomedov presents dangers at every distance. He is long, accurate with his kicks, difficult to hold down and capable of turning an opponent’s desperate takedown attempt into a submission opportunity.

Colgan cannot afford to become impatient. If he shoots from too far away or follows Nurmagomedov around the enclosure without cutting off his escape, the champion may pick him apart.

The challenger’s best chance is to crowd Nurmagomedov, keep him moving backward and turn the contest into a hard physical struggle. That is easier written than accomplished.

Colgan is 31, which makes this a test of the present rather than a promise for the future. There is little time for a gradual climb through the ratings.

A victory would make him an internationally important lightweight champion overnight. A strong showing in defeat could still improve his standing. But this is the bout that will tell us whether Colgan is merely a sound contender or a man capable of defeating the best.

## TATIANA POSTARNAKOVA

### The Hidden Prospect

TATIANA POSTARNAKOVA may be the most interesting young fighter on the preliminary card.

The 26-year-old flyweight has won all seven of her professional contests. Her opponent, Montana De La Rosa, brings a much less attractive record at 13-9-1, but those figures do not tell the whole story.

De La Rosa has operated against stronger and more experienced competition. She is accustomed to clinches, takedown battles and prolonged scrambles—the very situations that often expose an unbeaten prospect.

Postarnakova will not gain much merely by winning a cautious decision. What matters is how she handles the veteran’s grappling, whether she can escape bad positions and whether she can impose her own game after the first plan has been interrupted.

A convincing triumph would move Postarnakova much closer to the leading PFL flyweights and make her a possible future rival for Ditcheva.

## SEAN GAUCI

### Speed, Punches and One Big Question

SEAN GAUCI has reeled off eight consecutive victories and carries an 11-1 record into his meeting with Allan Begosso.

Gauci is a quick bantamweight who likes to work in combinations. Once he finds the proper distance, he can increase his output rapidly and overwhelm an opponent before the latter has time to recover.

His recent victory over former Cage Warriors champion Liam Gittins was a useful addition to his record and suggested that Gauci is ready for better company.

There remains one flaw to watch.

Gauci can become careless while attacking. He sometimes remains in range after completing a combination and may leave openings while pursuing a finish. Those liberties can be taken against ordinary opposition. They become costly against ranked men.

Begosso is a reasonable next assignment. Gauci should have the advantage in speed and variety, but the manner in which he conducts himself will be important.

A sharp, disciplined victory should earn him a match with one of the better bantamweights in the organization.

## MOUSTAPHA DIAKHATÉ

### The Raw Light-Heavyweight

MOUSTAPHA DIAKHATÉ is undefeated, powerfully built and equipped with the sort of physical tools that immediately command attention in the light-heavyweight class.

He has size, strength and genuine hitting power. When he attacks with confidence, he can make an opponent’s evening a short and unpleasant one.

Darryl Walker has accepted the assignment on the revised card, making this more of a showcase for Diakhaté than a complete examination of his ability.

That does not mean the bout is without interest.

Against Walker, attention should be paid to Diakhaté’s balance, patience and punch selection. A young power hitter can often become wild when an opponent refuses to fall at once. The best prospects do not merely hit hard; they remain orderly while doing it.

Diakhaté possesses one of the more intriguing physical ceilings on the program. Before he can be regarded as a contender, however, he must meet opponents capable of testing his defense and forcing him beyond his first attack.

## LAZARO DAYRON

### Prospect or Immediate Contender?

LAZARO DAYRON is 33 years old, which is rather advanced for a fighter still being called a prospect.

His record, however, demands attention.

Dayron is 10-0-1 and has used a strong wrestling attack to move toward the front of the PFL bantamweight picture. In his latest victory he completed seven takedowns, and he previously fought the difficult Danny Sabatello to a draw.

His New York opponent, Raufeon Stots, is the best measuring stick on the undercard.

Stots is a former interim Bellator champion and a complete, seasoned bantamweight. He can wrestle, scramble, box and make tactical adjustments during a contest. He has seen nearly every style and will not be surprised by Dayron’s first line of attack.

Because of Dayron’s age, there is no reason to move him slowly.

Should he defeat Stots in convincing fashion, the “prospect” label ought to be discarded. He would deserve immediate consideration among the promotion’s leading title challengers.

## LEVAN KHABALAEV

### A Long Lightweight With Finishing Intent

LEVAN KHABALAEV stands 6-foot-2, an unusual height for a lightweight, and has built his early record around quick finishes.

His reach gives him obvious possibilities. A tall lightweight who can strike effectively at the end of his punches and kicks is a troublesome proposition. Height alone, however, has never won a championship.

Chamy Delva is experienced enough to make Khabalaev answer several useful questions.

Can the tall man maintain his distance when crowded? Can he defend takedowns without surrendering position? Can he keep a steady pace if the early finish does not come?

Khabalaev remains one of the least proven fighters on the card, but he may also be one of the more interesting long-range investments in the lightweight division.

This is the sort of preliminary contest that can introduce a future contender—or expose a fighter whose early record was built against opposition unable to test him.

## THREE QUESTIONS TO BE ANSWERED

THE principal fighter to watch is Amru Magomedov. He appears to possess the best combination of age, skill, finishing ability and championship potential.

Archie Colgan faces the most important test. One victory could completely alter his position in the lightweight world.

Dakota Ditcheva has the greatest possibilities as an attraction. Her ability is already established; the matter now is whether she can remain healthy, active and visible enough to become one of the promotion’s central figures.

Postarnakova is the best concealed prospect on the preliminary bill. Dayron has the strongest veteran examination. Gauci is approaching ranked competition. Diakhaté has the raw materials, while Khabalaev remains an intriguing question mark.

PFL New York may be headed by a championship contest, but the future of several divisions will be operating farther down the bill.

Keep an eye on the youngsters.

One or two may not remain youngsters for long.
