**Renato Moicano** is a pretty damn good grappler, especially when he gets somebody's back, but I think people sometimes overlook his striking. He's got a nice jab, mixes in kicks, and is perfectly comfortable spending time on the feet. He isn't the most explosive guy at lightweight, and he can be a little hittable, but he's experienced, knows how to work his way into good positions, and is particularly dangerous with the rear-naked choke.


**Tom Nolan** is a big southpaw for lightweight who likes to pressure and throw in combination. He's got a sharp straight left, does some good work to the body and mixes in kicks, too. He definitely has power, but I like that he doesn't have to rely on it. We've seen him slow things down, make adjustments, and win a decision when the finish isn't there. He's still young, but there's quite a bit to like about his game.

**Randy Brown** is a tall, lanky welterweight who's pretty good at making use of his length. He's got a nice jab, throws straight punches and knees up the middle, and can be tricky to get close to when he's working at his preferred range. He's a capable grappler, too, although I think he's at his best when he can keep things standing and pick his shots. He can get caught when exchanges get messy, but there's a lot of experience behind what he does.


**Jacobe Smith** is a powerful southpaw with a serious wrestling background, and I think that's what makes him such a difficult guy to deal with. He doesn't have to get particularly fancy on the feet because he hits hard, closes distance quickly, and can chain takedowns together when he needs to. He's very explosive, and if he puts you on your back, he's not content to just hang out there. He'll let his hands go and do some damage.
