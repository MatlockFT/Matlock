**Renato Moicano** is a pretty damn good grappler, especially when he gets somebody's back, but I think people sometimes overlook his striking. He's got a nice jab, mixes in kicks, and is perfectly comfortable spending time on the feet. He isn't the most explosive guy at lightweight, and he can be a little hittable, but he's experienced, knows how to work his way into good positions, and is particularly dangerous with the rear-naked choke.


**Tom Nolan** is a big southpaw for lightweight who likes to pressure and throw in combination. He's got a sharp straight left, does some good work to the body and mixes in kicks, too. He definitely has power, but I like that he doesn't have to rely on it. We've seen him slow things down, make adjustments, and win a decision when the finish isn't there. He's still young, but there's quite a bit to like about his game.

**Randy Brown** is a tall, lanky welterweight who's pretty good at making use of his length. He's got a nice jab, throws straight punches and knees up the middle, and can be tricky to get close to when he's working at his preferred range. He's a capable grappler, too, although I think he's at his best when he can keep things standing and pick his shots. He can get caught when exchanges get messy, but there's a lot of experience behind what he does.


**Jacobe Smith** is a powerful southpaw with a serious wrestling background, and I think that's what makes him such a difficult guy to deal with. He doesn't have to get particularly fancy on the feet because he hits hard, closes distance quickly, and can chain takedowns together when he needs to. He's very explosive, and if he puts you on your back, he's not content to just hang out there. He'll let his hands go and do some damage.

She isn't the cleanest striker, and she's not much of a finishing threat, but she's physically strong and has a ton of experience against good competition. **Luana Santos** has a strong judo background, and you can see it in the way she likes to close distance and get her hands on people. She'll strike with you, but I think her best work comes when she can tie somebody up, hit a trip or takedown, and start looking for submissions. She's not the busiest striker out there, although she's fairly composed on the feet. Her grappling is definitely the more interesting part of her game, especially when she's able to get on top and start working.

There's plenty of offense there, but his defense is a concern, especially when he's exchanging in the pocket.


**Mickey Gall** is a jiu-jitsu black belt whose best work is taking the back and attacking rear-naked chokes. Six of his seven professional wins have come that way. His striking has never really caught up with his grappling, and he hasn't always had the wrestling to get better opponents to the ground. He's lost four straight and hasn't fought since November 2024.

**Jean-Paul Lebosnoyani** is a BJJ black belt who's been training since he was a kid, but there's quite a bit more to his game than submissions. He's got some power in his hands, can wrestle, and has a good variety of attacks on the ground, particularly his triangle chokes. Moving up to welterweight seems to have been a good decision for him. He hasn't lost since making the move, and he's won his first two UFC fights, although both went to decision.


**Farman Hasanov** has a very good freestyle wrestling background, and that's clearly the foundation of his game. He attempted 27 takedowns against Eric Nolan in his UFC debut and landed 17 of them, which is pretty ridiculous for a three-round fight. He doesn't throw a whole lot on the feet, at least not based on what we've seen in the UFC, but he does have four knockout wins on his record. I'm interested to see how his wrestling holds up against somebody who can really grapple.

He's coming off two wins, including a first-round submission of Erik Silva and a decision over Lucas Brennan.


**Dakota Hope** is an aggressive striker who throws a lot of volume and doesn't mind getting into exchanges. He's got five knockouts on his record, and he does a fair amount of work in the clinch, too. His UFC debut against Kai Kamaka III was a very competitive fight, even though he lost the split decision. He landed plenty of shots but took quite a few himself, and that's something he'll need to clean up as he faces better competition.

**Rodrigo Sezinando** is a BJJ black belt who likes to get fights to the ground, but he's got some power in his hands, too. He made it to the final of The Ultimate Fighter 33 after beating Diego Bianchini and Jeff Creighton, although Daniil Donchenko knocked him out in the first round of the finale. I think his grappling is the stronger part of his game. His striking can get a little sloppy, and there's still a lot we don't know about how he'll handle better competition.


**Theodor Berggren** is a switch-stance striker who's finished all eight of his professional wins, five by knockout and three by submission. He's aggressive, throws with some power and doesn't seem particularly interested in leaving things to the judges. He's dangerous when he gets going, but he can be caught himself. He was submitted by Justin Burlinson on the regional circuit, and Daniil Donchenko knocked him out with a head kick in his UFC debut.

**Julian Erosa** is a high-volume southpaw who fights with a lot of pressure and isn't afraid to get into ugly exchanges. He throws plenty of punches and kicks, but one of the better parts of his game is how quickly he recognizes a submission opening. He's particularly good with guillotines and D'Arce chokes, and he caught Ricardo Ramos and Christian Rodriguez with guillotines in back-to-back fights. His defense is a pretty obvious concern, though. He's been knocked out several times, most recently by Lerryan Douglas in March.


**JeongYeong Lee** has some good boxing, particularly his straight right and counterpunching, but he's also a BJJ black belt with three armbar victories on his record. He had a couple of impressive quick finishes in Road to UFC, knocking out Lu Kai in 42 seconds and submitting Xie Bin in 36. His UFC run hasn't been quite as convincing. He beat Blake Bilder, then got knocked out by Hyder Amil and lost a decision to Daniel Santos. He's been out since May 2025, so there's also a fair bit of inactivity coming into this one.

**Nick Klein** comes from a wrestling background and does most of his best work on the ground. His last three wins were all by rear-naked choke, including a 37-second finish on Contender Series that got him into the UFC. The problem has been getting his game going against better competition. Mansur Abdul-Malik and Andrey Pulyaev both stopped him in the second round, and his striking defense is something he still needs to address.


**Joseph Kropschot** is a BJJ black belt with six submission wins, including a rear-naked choke over David Allakhverdiev to win the LFA middleweight title. What I liked about his Contender Series fight with Jon Kunneman was that he didn't have to rely on the grappling. He used his reach well, landed a lot of jabs and teep kicks, and nearly finished an armbar late in the third round. He ended up winning a decision, but I thought he showed quite a bit in that fight. Allakhverdiev was 7–0 and Kunneman was 10–0 before those fights. Earlier losses came against Orion Cosce, Cody Brundage and Daniel Compton, so those last two wins are a much better indication of the level he's competing at now.

**Talita Alencar** is a six-time jiu-jitsu world champion, and her grappling is obviously the strongest part of her game. She's persistent with her takedowns, does a good job maintaining top control and is particularly dangerous when she gets to somebody's back. All four of her professional submission wins have come by rear-naked choke. Her striking is still a work in progress, but she's won three straight, including a late submission of Ariane Carnelossi. Those recent wins are worth more than the early finishes on her regional record. Carnelossi was 15–3 and Julia Polastri was 14–5 when Alencar fought them.


**Piera Rodriguez** has a boxing background, but her wrestling has done a lot of the work in the UFC. She throws in combination and does some good work in the clinch, particularly with her knees and elbows, but she's also pretty good at taking people down and keeping them there. She took Josefine Knutsson down eight times and Ketlen Souza seven times, and the UFC currently ranks her first at strawweight for takedown accuracy. She's got five knockout wins, but those all came before the UFC. All five of her UFC wins have been by decision, and she's now won three straight since that disqualification against Ariane Carnelossi.

**Lucia Szabova** is making her UFC debut after winning both the bantamweight and flyweight titles in OKTAGON. She's got a judo and Muay Thai background, but she's just as dangerous on the ground. She's got some good armbars, does damage from top position, and finished Cecilie Bolander and Leidiane Fernandes with ground strikes to win those belts. She also went five rounds with former UFC fighter Lucie Pudilová in August and won a decision. Pudilová came in with a 17–11 record, making her the most experienced opponent Szabova has faced. She's looked very good, especially since returning to MMA in 2024 after nearly three years away, but we'll see how that translates against UFC competition.


**Tainara Lisboa** has two Muay Thai world titles, but she's got more grappling than you might expect from that background. She likes working with kicks and elbows, particularly in the clinch, and she's won three MMA fights by submission, including a rear-naked choke over Jessica-Rose Clark in her UFC debut. She won her first two UFC fights, then missed about 19 months after a serious knee injury that required cartilage surgery. She's lost both fights since returning, first getting submitted by Luana Santos and then stopped by Melissa Croden. She's also dropping from bantamweight to flyweight for this fight, so we'll see how that goes.
